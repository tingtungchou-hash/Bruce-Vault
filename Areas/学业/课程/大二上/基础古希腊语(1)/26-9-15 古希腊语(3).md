# Parts of Speech 
Catalogories of words according to their function in the sentence (nouns, verbs, pronouns, articles, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, etc.) 
## Noun (ὄνομα/nomen) 
- proper noun 
- grammatical gender: in Greek, masculine, feminine, neuter
- inflection of nouns: **declension** 
- number: in Greek, singular, dual, plural 
- case: indicates the grammatical function of the noun (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, vocative) 
	- ὁ ἄνθρωπος ἄρτον ἐσθίει 
	- ἄρτον ὁ ἄνθρωπος ἐσθίει (√) 
	- ὁ ἄρτος ἄνθρωπον ἐσθίει (×) 
## Preposition (πρόθεσις/praepositio) 
- expresses the relation between a noun or pronoun on the one hand and another word on the other hand 
- prepositions are accompanied by the oblique cases: cases except the nominative (gen. dat. acc.) 
## O-declension Nouns (2nd. declension) 
quoted from [[Mastronarde Greek - Unit 3 笔记#o-declension]]
	![[Mastronarde Greek - Unit 3 笔记#o-declension]]
- **\[EXTENSION]** the circumflex on the genitive and dative endings is a by-product of contraction
	- the endings of the genitive and dative are long, so an acute that falls on the ultima is written as a circumflex; in the gen. sg., dat. sg. and gen. pl. that length is itself produced by contraction, i.e. the thematic vowel -ο- fused with the case ending, and a syllable that arose by contraction takes the circumflex when accented
	- gen. sg. -ου < earlier -οο (cf. the Homeric ending -οιο); the two omicrons contract into the spurious diphthong ου, hence στρατός → στρατοῦ
	- dat. sg. -ῳ < -οι, whose iota ceased to be pronounced and was written beneath the vowel (iota subscript), hence στρατός → στρατῷ
	- gen. pl. -ων contracted in the same way (thematic vowel plus the original ending of the genitive plural), hence στρατός → στρατῶν
	- the dat. pl. -οις and the dual -οιν carry the circumflex simply because they too are long (genuine diphthongs), which is why the special rule covers the genitive and dative of all numbers
