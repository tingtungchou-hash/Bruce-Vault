# Ancient Greek Language 
## Main characteristics
- An Indo-European language 
- The introduction of the Greek language in Greece was a result of the migration of Greek people
	- Achaean migration from north or east between 2000 and 1500 BCE 
		- Between 1600 and 1200/1100 BCE, the flourishing of Myceneaen civilisation 
		- Linear B & Lenear A 
	- Dorian migration at the end of the Myceneaen civilisation (late bronze age collapse)
	- Thus, the Greek language was characterised by variety of dialects with different layers of language connected to migrations, the subjects being Ionic-Attic, Achaean, Doric, etc.
- Around 350BC, the Koine Greek was adopted as the general language of Greece, and spread with the conquest of Alexander. 
## The alphabet 
- late 9th or early 8th century BCE, the Greeks adopted the letters of the Phoenician language and devised letters for vowels. Some letters were lost in the Attic Greek, e.g. vau and koppa (though *koppa was still used to indicate the number 90*) Nota bene that different letters had different values and prononciations in the age of dialects. 
- during the fourth century the twenty-for letter Ionian alphabet was adopted by Athenes, and this alphabet (the Ionian alphabet or the New Attic alphabet) won acceptence. The miniscule letters are from medieval manuscripts, while the capital letters are mainly from inscriptions.
