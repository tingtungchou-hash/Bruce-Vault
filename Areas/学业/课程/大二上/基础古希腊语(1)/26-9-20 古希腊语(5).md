# Perliminaries 
## verb (ῥῆμα, verbum) 
- affirms or predicate *an action* or a *state of being* 
- characteristics
	- they are inflicted to create finite forms (i.e. conjucations, as declension for nouns) 
	- non-finite forms of verbs include: 
		- verbal nouns (infinitive, gerund) 
		- verbal adjectives (particles)
	- there are 5 respects relating to a verb
		- tense
			- time: present, imperfect, future, aorist, perfect, pluperfect, future perfect
			- aspect: the way in which the action in the verb is viewed or ragarded 
				- e.g. I *learn* ancient Greek. vs I *am learning* ancient Greek 
		- mood: the manner in which an action or state of being is conceived
			- indicative: facts 
				- e.g. Students *attend* the ancient Greek course on Tuesday. 
			- subjunctive: assumption and possibility 
				- e.g. The general orders that the soldiers *be* taken captives. 
			- optative: wish and possibility 
				- e.g. If only the general _would take_ the soldiers captive.
			- imparative: command
				- e.g. Do as I said!  
		- voice: active, middle, passive  
		- number: 1, 2, 3+  
		- person: 1st, 2nd, 3rd 
- **principal parts** 
	- 1st sg ind. act. pres.; 1st sg ind. act. fut.; 1st sg ind. act. aorist; 1st sg ind. act. perf.; 1st sg ind. mid./pass. perf.; 1st sg ind. pass. aorist
	- e.g. λύω, λύσω, ἔλυσα, λέλυκα, λέλυμαι, ἐλύθην 
## thematic verbs (ω-verbs) and athematic verbs (μι-verbs) 
- thematic verbs: stem - theme vowel - endings (note that not all theme vowels are perceivable because phonetic transitions occurred)
- athematic verbs: stem - endings
