# The legal formation of ancient Rome 
- 149 BC lex Calpurnia 
- 123/2 BC lex repetundarum ... 
## before the court 
- Provincials of Hispania protested the iniuria they suffered, and asked for the return of at least the wealth plundered, reaching some kind of success
> 	ii de migistratum Romanorum avaritia superbiaque conquesti, nixi genibus ab snatu pertierunt, ne se socios foedius spoliari vexarique quam hostis patiantur. - Livy 
## devise and development of the court 
- In 149 BC, tribunus plebis L. Calpurnius Piso Prugi devised the **lex Calpurnia de pecuniis repetundis**, marking the beginning of the Roman court 
> 	... filius enim L. Pisonis erat, eius qui primus de pecuniis repetundis legem tulit. - Cicero, in Verrem, 4.56 
- the Gracchi and the 123/2 BC lex repetundarum (tabula Bemtina), and the meticulous rules of appointing patroni, accusation, etc, was still used by the senators to hinder and delay the conduct of the cae
> 	... quoi socium nominisue **Latini exterarumue nationum** ... ablatum captum coactum conciliatum auersumnue siet, de ea re eius petitio nominisque delatio esto ... 
- NOTA BENE that due to the aforementioned nature of the Roman court, the cases were de facto ==a ***spectacle*** talored to the needs of the extremely wealthy aristocracy both Roman and non-Roman==, a conduct almost having nothing to do with common provincials, which was indeed the reason while governors continued to exploit provincials despite erected laws. That the case against Verres was possible was largely thanks to Cicero as their active and enthusiast patronus. 
> 	... quadringentiens sestertium ex Sicilia contra leges abustuliesse - in Verrem
- 45 BC lex Julia Municipiarum
