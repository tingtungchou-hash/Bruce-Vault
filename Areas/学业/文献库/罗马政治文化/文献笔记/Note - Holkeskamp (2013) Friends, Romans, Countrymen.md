### Contio & the Rhetoric
1. the significance of the contiones
2. the rhetoric of emphatic direct address ("apud vos", in the servive of you, etc.)
> [[Holkeskamp_2013_Friends, Romans, Countrymen.pdf#page=5&selection=145,48,157,23|〇]]  This rhetoric of direct address represents, reproduces, and indeed creates a particular kind of inseparable interconnectivity, an implicit mutual understanding, consent, and consensus, and sometimes even a sort of complicity between the orator and his ego addressing the public on the one hand and this same public as addressee on the other
3. the rhetoric of inclusion 
	1. to engage the addressees as active co-constructors of the discourse 
	2. to present the *ego* of the orator (magisterial roles), and thereby his visibility, gnobilitas
	3. thus the rhetoric creates a kind of difference and asymmetry, the orator assuming leadership and guidance 
> [[Holkeskamp_2013_Friends, Romans, Countrymen.pdf#page=7&selection=102,70,110,23|〇]] This is to mean that the ego of the orator always (again at least implicitly, but often enough explicitly) claims the role of leadership and guidance

> [[Holkeskamp_2013_Friends, Romans, Countrymen.pdf#page=6&selection=251,0,272,94|〇]] This convention of addressing an audience—again at least implicitly—presupposes the opposite pole of the persona of the orator: his ego is the necessary complement of the ‘you’ of the contio.
