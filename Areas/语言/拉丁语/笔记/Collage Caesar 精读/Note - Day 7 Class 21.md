27.7 Quibus quoniam prō pietāte satisfēcerit, habēre nunc sē ratiōnem officī prō beneficiīs Caesaris: monēre, ōrāre Titūrium prō hospitiō, ut suae ac mīlitum salūtī cōnsulat. 8 . Magnam manum Germānōrum conductam Rhēnum trānsīsse; hanc adfore bīduō. 9 . Ipsōrum esse cōnsilium velintne priusquam fīnitimī sentiant ēductōs ex hībernīs mīlitēs aut ad Cicerōnem aut ad Labiēnum dēdūcere, quōrum alter mīlia passuum circiter quinquaginta, alter paulō amplius ab eīs absit. 10 . Illud sē pollicērī et iūreiūrandō cōnfirmāre tūtum iter per fīnēs datūrum; 11 . quod cum faciat, et cīvitātī sēsē cōnsulere, quod hībernīs levētur, et Caesarī prō eius meritīs grātiam referre. Hāc ōrātiōne habitā discēdit Ambiorīx. 28.1 Arpinēius et Iūnius, quae audīerunt, ad lēgātōs dēferunt. Illī repentīnā rē perturbātī, etsi ab hoste ea dīcēbantur, tamen nōn neglegenda exīstimābant maximēque hāc rē permovēbantur, quod cīvitātem īgnōbilem atque humilem Eburōnum suā sponte populō Rōmānō bellum facere ausam vix erat crēdendum. 2 . Itaque ad cōnsilium rem dēferunt magnaque inter eōs exsistit contrōversia. 3 . Lucius Aurunculēius complūrēsque tribūnī mīlitum et prīmōrum ōrdinum centuriōnēs nihil temerē agendum neque ex hībernīs iniūssū Caesaris discēdendum exīstimābant: quantāsvīs magnās etiam cōpiās Germānōrum sustinērī posse mūnītīs hībernīs docēbant: rem esse testimōniō, quod prīmum hostium impetum multīs ultrō vulneribus illātis fortissimē sustinuerint: rē frumentāriā non premī; intereā et ex proximīs hībernīs et ā Caesare conventūra subsidia: postrēmō quid esse levius aut turpius quam, auctōre hoste, dē summīs rēbus capere cōnsilium?

## 英文翻译
XXVII 
⑦ Since he had completed enough of these out of piety, 
he now had a regard of his duty for the good deeds of Caesar: 
he said he pleaded to Titurius on behalf of hospitality, 
that he consider the safety of himself and his soldiers. 

⑧ a great ~~assembled~~ ***hired*** army of the Germans had crossed over the Rhine; 
This would be present in two days. 

⑨ the plan is their own whether they retreat the soldiers out of the winter camp to either Cicero or Labienus, of whom one is about 50 miles, and the other was little more away from them. 

⑩ he promised it and confirmed by a swear that he would give a guarded journey through his borders. 
⑪ with that all done, he would ~~tell his community why~~ ***consider for his community for (the burden of)*** the winter camps were lifted, and convey gratitude to Caesar for his merits. 
After having this speech, Ambiorix went away. 

XXVIII
① Arpineius and Iunius, refered what they heared to the legates. 
They, confused by the sudden message, although the things were told to them by the enemy, still they estimated not to neglect it, and were moved by the thing to a great extent, because it is hard for them to believed that that an unkown and humble city of the Eburoni had dared to wage war against the Roman people at their own will. 

② And they reported the thing ~~about the plan~~ ***to the council***, and there existed great disagreement among themselves. 

③ Lucius Aurunculeius and many military tribunes and centurions of the first lines thought that nothing should be done without caution, and that they shouln't leave without the order of Caesar away from their winter camps. 

They told that no matter how great the armies of Germans are, they are able to survive their attack with the winter camps fortified. 
This thing serves as a testimony that they'll resist most courageously the first way of enemy assult and even more with many enemies neutralised from the fight by wounds. 
The affairs of corns and supplies wasn't a pressing issue, for both from the vicinity of the winter camps and from Caesar supplies will come. 
At last what is lighter or uglier than to ~~take a piece of advice~~ ***to make a decision*** of the most important things, with the initiator being an enemy?
