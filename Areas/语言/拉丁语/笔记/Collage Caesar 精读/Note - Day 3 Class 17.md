## 语法分析
XIX 
① Virī, 
==quantās== pecūniās ab uxōribus \[dōtis nōmine] **accēpērunt**, 
==tantās== \[ex suīs bonīs] \[aestimātiōne factā], 
**cum** dōtibus **==commūnicant==**.

② Huius omnis pecūniae \[coniūnctim] => ratiō habētur 
==fructūs==**que** servāntur: 
uter eōrum vītā superāvit, 
\[ad eum] pars utrīusque \[cum frūctibus superiōrum temporum] **pervenit**. 

③ Virī in uxōrēs, ==sīcutī== in līberōs, vītae necisque **habent** potestātem; 
	**et** **cum** paterfamiliae illūstriōre locō ==nātus== **dēcessit**, 
		eius propinquī **conveniunt** 
	**et**,\[dē morte] **sī** rēs in suspiciōnem **venit**, 
		\[dē uxōribus] in servīlem modum quaestiōnem **habent** 
	**et**, **sī** ==compertum== **est**, 
		ignī atque omnibus ==tormentīs== excruciātās ==**interficiunt**==. 

④ Fūnera sunt \[prō cultū Gallōrum] magnifica et sūmptuōsa; 
omnia**que** <= (quae vīvīs ==cordī== fuisse arbitrantur) in ignem īnferunt, 
etiam animālia, ac \[paulō suprā hanc memoriam] servī et clientēs, 
	<= (quōs ab eīs ==dīlēctōs== esse ==constābat==), 
\[iustīs fūneribus cōnfectīs] ūnā cremābantur.

XX
① Quae cīvitātēs commodius suam rem pūblicam administrāre **exīstimantur**, 
habent lēgibus sānctum, 
	**sī** quis quid \[dē rē pūblicā] \[ā fīnitimīs] \[rūmōre aut fāmā] accēperit, 
**utī** 
ad magistrātum **dēferat** 
**nēve** cum quō aliō **commūnicet**, 

② quod saepe (hominēs temerāriōs atque imperītōs) \[falsīs rūmōribus] terrērī 
	**et** ad facinus impellī 
	**et** \[dē summīs rēbus] cōnsilium capere 
cognitum est. 

③ Magistrātūs quae ==vīsa sunt== **occultant** 
quae**que** esse ex ūsū iūdicāvērunt multitūdinī **prōdunt**. 
\[Dē rē pūblicā] nisi per cōncilium loquī nōn ==**concēditur**==.

## 英文翻译
XIX 
① Husbands receive 
so much money from their wives in the name of dowry, 
as much ~~for his goodness~~ ***from his possession***, with estimation done, 
~~since the couple shares the dowry~~ ***and share with the dowry***. (~~subjunctive~~)

② The calculation and usage of this whole lot of money are conducted jointly, 
and the profit is taken care of: 
if either of the two survive the other's death, 
the money of ~~the other~~ ***of both sides, together with previous profit***  belongs to him 

③ Husbands have the power of life and death over their wives, as they do over their children. 
And, when the paterfamilias born into a more illustrious position passed away, 
his kinsmen will convine. 
And, if the death issue came into suspicion, 
they'll conduct an investigation on the wives in the manner of slaves. 
And, if it (the crime) was found out, 
they'll kill the tortured guilty ones with fire and all torture.

④ The funerals are magnificent and sumptuous in Gallic culture. 
And, all who ***or what*** they think to have been dear to the living, they threw into fire, 
and even animal and slaves and clients a little before this memory, 
who it is agreed to be ~~chosen~~ ***loved*** by them, are all burnt together, after the funeral is completed. 

XX
① The communities which they judge to administrate their own states properly, 
have it ordained by laws 
that if anyone receives anything about the state from the neighbouring states through rumours and hearsay, (s)he should report it to the magistrate, and not share it with any other people, 
②because it is well-kown that often people inconsiderate and ~~scared~~ ***inexperienced*** are terrified by faulse rumors, are impelled into crime, and prepare plans about the most important affairs. 

③ Magistrates conceal those seeming to be the best, 
and give to the people what he judge to be of use. 
It is not allowed to talk about public affairs unless through the assembly.
