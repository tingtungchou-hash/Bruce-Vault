# habeō, habēre, habuī, habitus 2.

## 基本信息
- **语言**: 拉丁语
- **词性**: v. a. (第二变位)
- **词源**: 对应梵语 gabhasti (手/前臂), 古爱尔兰语 gaibim (我拿)

## 释义

1. Perf. subj. habessit, Cic. Leg. 2, 8, 19; inf. haberier, Plaut. Mil. 2, 6, 111), v. a. and n. etym. dub.; cf. Gr. κώπη, handle; Lat. capio; Germ. haben, Haft; Engl. have, to have, in the widest sense of the word, to hold, keep, possess, cherish, entertain, occupy, enclose, contain (cf.: teneo, possideo, etc.).

2. In gen.

3. Of personal subjects.
   a. With persons or things as objects: SI INTESTATO MORITVR, CVI SVVS HERES NEC SIT, AGNATVS PROXIMVS FAMILIAM HABETO, Fragm. XII. Tab. ap. Ulp. 4. In partic.

5. Pregn., to have or possess property (mostly absol.): miserum istuc verbum et pessumum'st, habuisse et nihil habere,Plaut. Rud. 5, 2, 34; cf. Ter.    a. With an object- or relative-clause, to have the means, ability, or knowledge, i. e. to be in a condition, to be able, to know how to do or say any thing.
   a. To hold, use, wield, handle, manage: nec inmensa barbarorum scuta, enormis hastas, inter truncos arborum perinde haberi quam pila,Tac. A. 2, 14.—Trop.: quo modo rem publicam habuerint (majores), disserere,Sall. C. 5, 9; cf.: reipublicae partes,Tac. A. 4, 6 init.
