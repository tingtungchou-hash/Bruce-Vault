# Greek alphabet 

α β γ δ ε ζ η θ ι κ λ μ ν ξ ο π ρ σ(ς) τ υ φ χ ψ ω 
## long and short vowels

- short vowels ᾰ, ε, ῐ, ο, ῠ, final οι & αι
- long vowels ᾱ, ει, η, ῑ, ου, ω, ῑ 
- diphthong αι, ει, ευ, ηυ, οι, ου

## elision and crasis 

- for elision, omit the last vowel and add "᾿" instead 
	- άλλ==ὰ== ὠπελήσω => ἀλλ==᾿==ώφελήσω
- if one word ends with **π, τ, κ**, and the other starts with **rough breathing**, π => φ, τ=>θ, κ => χ 
	- ὑ==π==ὸ ==ὑ==μῶν +> ὑ==φ=='ὑμῶν

- for crasis, merge the two vowels and add "᾿" to the vowel formed by the contraction 
	- τὸ ἔλαττον => το==ὔ==λαττον, προ έδοσαν => προ==ὔ==δοσαν
- if the **first vowel** in crasis is a form of the article with rough breathing, add "῾" instead. 
	- ὁ αὐτός => αὑτός
- if the **second vowel** in crasis has a rough breathing, transfer it to the first vowel 
	- καὶ ὁ πόνος => χὠ πόνος, κ=>χ

# Accentuation 
## acute, grave, and circumflex 

- acute: average mark for accent 
- grave: U-accented words appearing with others 
	- ὁδ==ό==ς => ὁδ==ὸ==ς καί ...
- circumflex
	- some declension of words with accented U 
		- στρατηγός => στρατηγ==οῦ==
	- P-accented words, when P is long and U is short 
		- ==οἶ==κ==ο==ς

## proclictic and enclitic 
### proclitics 

οὐ εἰ ὡς εἰς ἐν ἐκ ὁ ἡ οἱ αἱ 
- οὐ => οὔ at the end of a sentence 
- a prolictic receives an acute accent when it is followed by an enclitic 

### enclitics

- U accented => keep circumflex or acute, acute not changed into a grave
	- ἀγαθός τις, ἀγαθῷ τῳ, τῶν ἀγαθῶν ἐστι 
- a disyllabic enclitic receives an accent on its second syllable. 
- a word with a **circumflex on P** or an **acute on A** receives an extra accent on **U** 
	- δῶρόν τι, ἄνθρωπός τις
